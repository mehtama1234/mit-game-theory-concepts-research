Lecture 22: Signaling

Studies how privately informed senders can influence receivers through observable actions that may reveal type.

Open YouTube lecture

What This Lecture Teaches

The lecture studies how a privately informed sender can affect a receiver's beliefs through observable action. The action is informative only when types have different incentives to imitate it.

Where The Math Enters

The math enters through sender types, signal choices, receiver beliefs, receiver actions, and incentive constraints that stop one type from profitably mimicking another.

The mathematical object has to exist because dynamic private-information games require beliefs and actions to discipline each other. Strategies say what players do; beliefs say what they infer after histories. PBE binds the two so credibility and updating are checked together. The mathematical object has to exist because observers infer hidden type from costly or informative action. A signaling model must represent sender types, signal costs, receiver beliefs, and receiver responses together. Otherwise the analyst cannot check whether imitation destroys the message.

Worked Mini-Example

Education can signal ability if high-ability workers find education less costly or more worthwhile. If low-ability workers would copy the same education level, the signal pools rather than separates.

Mistakes To Avoid

The mistake is to think costly automatically means informative. A signal works only if the wrong type does not want to imitate it under the receiver's response.

Students often get stuck by solving the strategy profile and treating beliefs as commentary. In PBE, beliefs are part of the equilibrium data, especially because off-path beliefs can affect credibility. Students often get stuck by assuming costly means informative. A costly signal is informative only if the cost or benefit differs across types in the right direction.

How To Recognize This Later

Look for dynamic asymmetric-information models with off-path beliefs, updating, and sequential rationality. Look for actions that are valuable mainly because observers infer hidden quality, commitment, or type from them.

Concepts In This Lecture

Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium

Problem: The problem is dynamic play with hidden information: after observing actions, players must update beliefs and continue choosing rationally.

Mathematical handle: The object is a strategy profile plus a belief system, with sequential rationality and belief consistency where Bayes' rule applies.

What breaks without it: Without PBE, signaling and other dynamic asymmetric-information models could prescribe actions without explaining the beliefs that make those actions rational. Credibility would be separated from learning.

Open concept page

Signaling

Problem: The problem is how someone with private information can convince others of it through an observable action.

Mathematical handle: The object is a sender-receiver game with types, signals, receiver beliefs, and receiver actions.

What breaks without it: Without signaling, the course could not explain education, warranties, investment, or other actions whose value partly comes from what observers infer. It would treat all messages as direct information rather than incentive-shaped evidence.
