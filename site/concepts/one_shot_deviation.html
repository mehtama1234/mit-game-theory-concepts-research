One-Shot Deviation Principle

A shortcut for checking whether changing one move at one point can improve a strategy.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is checking a whole dynamic strategy can be exhausting because a strategy describes what to do after many histories.

Why does this problem exist?

The one-shot deviation principle exists because, in the right finite or discounted settings, profitable complex deviations reveal themselves through a profitable single-period deviation.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Backward induction assigns optimal choices from terminal decision nodes back to the start. The useful relation is `V(node) = max_action V(next node).`. In plain language: V is continuation value; at each decision point the player chooses the next branch with the best continuation value.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. The concept is not a new equilibrium by itself. It is a proof tool for checking dynamic optimality, especially when directly checking every possible multi-period deviation would be impractical.

Worked Mini-Example

Instead of checking every possible plan for a repeated-game player, check whether cheating today and returning to the plan tomorrow pays. If even that one-step cheat fails everywhere, the larger plan may be safe.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to use the shortcut without checking the assumptions that make local deviations enough.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for dynamic incentive proofs that replace many possible deviations with a one-period deviation inequality.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. The principle matters because dynamic games can have enormous strategy spaces. It gives the course a tractable way to audit sequential rationality without listing every complicated alternative plan.
