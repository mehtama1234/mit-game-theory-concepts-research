Implicit Cartels

Firms may keep prices high without an explicit agreement when future punishment disciplines cheating.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is how firms might keep prices high without signing an explicit cartel agreement.

Why does this problem exist?

Implicit cartels exist because repeated market interaction can make cheating costly. A firm may avoid undercutting today because rivals can punish tomorrow.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A discount factor delta weights future payoffs in an intertemporal sum. The useful relation is `U = u_0 + delta u_1 + delta^2 u_2 + ...`. In plain language: u_t is payoff in period t; delta between 0 and 1 controls how strongly the future matters.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. This is why market transparency can cut two ways. Observing prices may help consumers, but it can also make deviations easier for rivals to detect and punish.

Worked Mini-Example

A gas station can cut price today and steal customers, but nearby stations may respond with a price war. Tacit collusion asks when fear of that future makes quiet cooperation stable.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to assume high prices prove explicit agreement. Repeated-game incentives can sometimes generate similar outcomes without a formal contract.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for industries with repeated price interaction, observability of deviations, and credible punishment paths.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. This application connects abstract repeated-game logic to market behavior. It shows why antitrust analysis has to care about observability, patience, number of firms, and punishment, not only explicit written agreements.
