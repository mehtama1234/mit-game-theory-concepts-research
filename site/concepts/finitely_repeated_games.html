Finitely Repeated Games

Playing a stage game a known limited number of times.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is whether repeating a game for a known number of rounds changes behavior relative to playing once.

Why does this problem exist?

Finite repetition exists because the end of the relationship can unravel incentives. If everyone knows the last round has no future punishment, that affects the round before it, and so on.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Backward induction assigns optimal choices from terminal decision nodes back to the start. The useful relation is `V(node) = max_action V(next node).`. In plain language: V is continuation value; at each decision point the player chooses the next branch with the best continuation value.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version is a lesson about known endings. Repeating a stage game may seem like it should allow punishment and trust, but if the final period is known, the last period has no later punishment. If the last period unravels to the one-shot equilibrium, the next-to-last period can lose its punishment power too, and the logic can roll backward.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is backward induction applied across periods. The terminal period anchors the continuation value, and earlier periods inherit that value. A fixed horizon can therefore turn future discipline into an empty promise near the end.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because a known horizon changes every period’s incentives. The model needs to preserve the number of remaining rounds so last-period logic can be pulled backward. A finite repeated game is not just many one-shot games; it is a time-indexed strategic object.

Why is this concept important?

Finitely repeated games matter because they teach a negative lesson: repeating a game does not automatically create cooperation. If the last round is known, the absence of future punishment can unravel incentives backward.

What breaks without it?

Without finite repetition, students might overgeneralize from ongoing relationships and assume history always disciplines behavior. The course needs the finite case to show why the endpoint is strategically powerful.

Worked Mini-Example

If two firms know they will compete for exactly ten days, cheating on day ten cannot be punished on day eleven. That can make day nine punishment less credible too.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by counting repetitions rather than asking whether the relationship has a strategic future at each date. Ten known rounds and an ongoing relationship are different objects.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is the known endpoint. The finite repeated-game unraveling logic changes when the horizon is uncertain, reputational types exist, or players cannot observe deviations. The classroom value of the finite case is that it blocks a lazy intuition about repetition. More rounds do not automatically mean more cooperation; what matters is whether each round still has a future consequence that can discipline current behavior. That backward pressure is the lecture’s main warning about finite horizons.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to assume repetition automatically creates cooperation. A known last round can destroy that logic.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for known horizons and unraveling arguments that start at the final period.

Connected Concepts

Finite repetition connects backward induction to infinite repetition. It is the contrast case that makes discounting and no-known-final-round assumptions meaningful later.

Worked Example Card

Setup: Two firms compete on price for exactly ten days, and both firms know the relationship ends after day ten.

Walkthrough: On day ten, cheating cannot be punished on day eleven because there is no day eleven. Once day ten cooperation loses discipline, day nine threats become weaker too. The logic can unravel backward through the finite horizon.

Lesson: A known endpoint can destroy future punishment before the endpoint arrives.

Trap: Do not assume repetition automatically creates cooperation when the ending is common knowledge.
