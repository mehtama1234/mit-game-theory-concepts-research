Common Knowledge

A fact that everyone knows, everyone knows everyone knows, and so on.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that coordinated action may require more than everyone privately knowing a fact. People may also need to know that others know it, and know that others know that others know it.

Why does this problem exist?

Common knowledge exists because higher-order uncertainty can block coordination. A public fact can support behavior that a private fact cannot.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Common knowledge means everyone knows, everyone knows everyone knows, and the chain continues. The useful relation is `E, KE, KKE, KKKE, ...`. In plain language: E is the event; K means known by the players; repeated K's represent higher-order knowledge.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version climbs from private knowledge to public coordination. If everyone knows a fact privately, coordination can still fail when each person is unsure whether others know it too. Common knowledge requires the chain to continue: everyone knows, everyone knows everyone knows, and so on. This is why public announcements can change behavior differently from identical private messages.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is recursive knowledge. Start with an event E. Apply the “everyone knows” operator repeatedly: everyone knows E, everyone knows that everyone knows E, and so on without a finite stopping point.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because coordination can depend on levels of knowledge, not only on the fact itself. Recursive knowledge operators let the course distinguish private knowledge from public assurance. That distinction explains why announcements can move behavior when private messages cannot.

Why is this concept important?

Common knowledge matters because coordination can require publicness, not just truth. A fact can be known by everyone individually but still fail to coordinate action if people are unsure whether others know it too.

What breaks without it?

Without common knowledge, the course would miss higher-order uncertainty. Players might all know the same event, yet hesitate because they do not know whether others know, or whether others know that they know.

Worked Mini-Example

If a policy change is announced publicly, firms may coordinate on the new rule. If each firm only hears it privately and doubts whether others heard it, behavior may not shift the same way.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck because the infinite chain sounds philosophical. The course uses it for concrete coordination: people act differently when a fact is publicly shared than when each person only privately receives it.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is public assurance. A fact can be true and widely known without becoming common knowledge if players doubt whether others received or understood it. The boundary is that common knowledge is stronger than public posting in the ordinary sense. If people miss the announcement, doubt its authenticity, or doubt whether others saw it, the recursive chain can fail.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to treat true, known, and common knowledge as the same. They are different levels of shared awareness.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for coordination failures, public announcements, or arguments that depend on what everyone knows about everyone else's knowledge.

Connected Concepts

Common knowledge connects iterated deletion, rationalizability, equilibrium selection, cheap talk, and public communication. It is the knowledge-side analogue of mutual consistency: everyone reasoning must line up at many levels.

Worked Example Card

Setup: Every firm privately receives the same rule change, but each firm is unsure whether others received it.

Walkthrough: Private knowledge may not move behavior because each firm worries others may still act under the old rule. A public announcement changes more than information; it tells everyone that everyone heard it. That recursive awareness can coordinate action.

Lesson: Common knowledge is the publicness structure that lets players reason about others' reasoning.

Trap: Do not confuse many people knowing a fact with everyone knowing that everyone knows it.
