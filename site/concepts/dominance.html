Dominance

A way to rule out choices that are never better than another choice.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that some choices are plainly bad no matter what the other side does. Keeping them in the analysis can distract from the strategic choices that actually need thought.

Why does this problem exist?

Dominance exists because comparison can sometimes be made without beliefs. If one action beats another across every possible opponent action, the worse action does not need a prediction about opponents to be rejected.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Dominance compares one strategy against another across possible opponent choices. The useful relation is `s dominates t if u_i(s, a_-i) >= u_i(t, a_-i) for all a_-i, with strict inequality somewhere.`. In plain language: The inequality checks every possible action by others; dominance means one strategy is never worse and sometimes better.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives.

Worked Mini-Example

If a firm earns less from pricing high than pricing low no matter what the rival does, high pricing is dominated in that model. The firm does not need to forecast the rival before rejecting it.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to delete a strategy because it feels unlikely. Dominance is a universal comparison, not a hunch.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for arguments that rule out actions before equilibrium is computed because one action is never a best answer.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. Dominance is powerful because it avoids speculation about beliefs, but that strength is also its limit. Many interesting strategic choices are not dominated; they survive because their value depends on what the player thinks others will do.
