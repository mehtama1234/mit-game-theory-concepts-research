Dominance

A way to rule out choices that are never better than another choice.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that some choices are plainly bad no matter what the other side does. Keeping them in the analysis can distract from the strategic choices that actually need thought.

Why does this problem exist?

Dominance exists because comparison can sometimes be made without beliefs. If one action beats another across every possible opponent action, the worse action does not need a prediction about opponents to be rejected.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Dominance compares one strategy against another across possible opponent choices. The useful relation is `s dominates t if u_i(s, a_-i) >= u_i(t, a_-i) for all a_-i, with strict inequality somewhere.`. In plain language: The inequality checks every possible action by others; dominance means one strategy is never worse and sometimes better.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version shows a rare moment where beliefs are unnecessary. To test dominance, compare one strategy with another against every possible opponent action. If one strategy is never worse and sometimes better, the inferior strategy has no reason to survive. The key is the universal quantifier: every contingency must be checked. This is why dominance is strong but limited. It can clean up a game before equilibrium reasoning, but many strategies are not dominated even though they may fail later tests.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is an inequality repeated across columns, states, or opponent actions. The concept says the comparison does not depend on a forecast. If the inequality holds everywhere, no belief about the opponent can rescue the dominated strategy.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because some choices can be rejected without forecasting the opponent. The dominance comparison formalizes “no matter what they do” as a set of inequalities across contingencies. That makes deletion a proof about payoffs rather than a guess about likely behavior.

Why is this concept important?

Dominance matters because it is one of the cleanest places where rationality has bite without requiring a forecast. If an action loses to another action in every relevant contingency, the course can reject it before solving the full strategic puzzle.

What breaks without it?

Without dominance, every listed strategy would look equally alive even when some choices have no defensible reason to be played. The analysis would be noisier and less disciplined before equilibrium reasoning even begins.

Worked Mini-Example

If a firm earns less from pricing high than pricing low no matter what the rival does, high pricing is dominated in that model. The firm does not need to forecast the rival before rejecting it.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by deleting strategies that are merely unattractive under their favorite prediction. That is not dominance. A strategy that is bad against one opponent move but useful against another is belief-dependent, not dominated.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is strength. Dominance is decisive when it applies, but many live strategies are not dominated. Those cases need beliefs, best responses, or equilibrium instead of more aggressive deletion.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to delete a strategy because it feels unlikely. Dominance is a universal comparison, not a hunch.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for arguments that rule out actions before equilibrium is computed because one action is never a best answer.

Connected Concepts

Dominance leads into iterated deletion and rationalizability, and it also prepares the ground for Nash because dominated strategies cannot be serious best responses. Its limit explains why beliefs later become necessary.

Worked Example Card

Setup: A firm chooses high price or low price while a rival chooses aggressive or calm behavior.

Walkthrough: Compare high price and low price under the rival's aggressive move, then compare them under the rival's calm move. If low price gives at least as much payoff in both comparisons and more in one, high price is dominated. The firm does not need a forecast about the rival to remove high price.

Lesson: Dominance is powerful because it is a no-matter-what comparison.

Trap: Do not delete an action because it feels unlikely; delete it only when the inequality survives every modeled contingency.
