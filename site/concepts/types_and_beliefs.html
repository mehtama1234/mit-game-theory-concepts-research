Types And Beliefs

Types store hidden information; beliefs describe what others think those types might be.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is keeping track of hidden facts and what players think those facts might be.

Why does this problem exist?

Types and beliefs exist because private information has to be represented somewhere. A type stores what one player knows; a belief stores what others think is likely.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A belief is a probability distribution over states, types, or histories. The useful relation is `E[u(a, theta)] = sum_theta p(theta) u(a, theta).`. In plain language: theta is the unknown type or state; p(theta) is its probability; expected utility averages payoffs across possibilities.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. Beliefs also change after observations in dynamic games. That is why this concept points forward to perfect Bayesian equilibrium, where beliefs must travel with strategies.

Worked Mini-Example

In an auction, a bidder's value is their type. Other bidders do not see it, but they hold beliefs about the distribution of possible values.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to blur type and action. A type is hidden information; an action is a choice that may reveal or conceal it.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for any model where behavior depends on values, costs, quality, or information that only one side observes.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. Types and beliefs are the grammar of asymmetric information. They let the course say both what is privately known and what is inferred by people who do not observe it directly.
