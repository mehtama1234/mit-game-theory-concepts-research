Study Route

A compact path through the course, from first choice primitives to evidence-backed strategic reasoning.

Study route

Start With Choice Before Strategy

Reader question: What does a player want before anyone else enters the story?

Plain-language goal: Learn why preferences, utility, and expected utility are the measuring tools underneath every later payoff table.

Study route

Represent The Strategic Situation

Reader question: What object keeps everyone's choices, timing, and payoffs visible?

Plain-language goal: Move from one-person choice to game representation: tables for simultaneous dependence, trees for time and information.

Study route

Make Predictions Survive Deviations

Reader question: Can each player look at the proposed outcome and still want to stay put?

Plain-language goal: Understand equilibrium as a mutual consistency test, then see why conflict and randomization require sharper versions of the same idea.

Study route

Test Time, Threats, And Patience

Reader question: Will a promised future action still make sense when the future arrives?

Plain-language goal: Use backward reasoning, subgame checks, bargaining logic, and repeated-game discipline to separate credible plans from empty announcements.

Study route

Reason With Hidden Information

Reader question: What changes when players know different values, costs, types, histories, or facts that other players cannot directly see?

Plain-language goal: Track types, beliefs, updating, signaling, cheap talk, and common knowledge without treating beliefs as arbitrary story glue.

Study route

Read Rules As Incentive Machines

Reader question: How do auction and market rules change what strategic people choose?

Plain-language goal: See mechanism and auction topics as design problems: the rule must make the desired behavior privately sensible.
