Lecture 21: Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium

Combines dynamic credibility with belief updating in games where histories can reveal private information and observed actions change what players infer.

Open YouTube lecture

What This Lecture Teaches

The lecture combines time with hidden information. Strategies must be sequentially rational, and beliefs must be specified and updated where possible, because histories can reveal private information.

Where The Math Enters

The math enters as a strategy profile plus a belief system. At each information set, actions must be optimal given beliefs, and beliefs must follow Bayes' rule on path.

The mathematical object has to exist because uncertainty has two sides: the hidden state and someone’s probability over it. Types represent the state; beliefs represent the uncertainty. Keeping them separate lets the course analyze signaling, updating, and type-contingent strategy without confusing truth with perception. The mathematical object has to exist because dynamic private-information games require beliefs and actions to discipline each other. Strategies say what players do; beliefs say what they infer after histories. PBE binds the two so credibility and updating are checked together.

Worked Mini-Example

After observing a price or signal, a buyer forms a belief about seller type and chooses whether to buy. PBE checks both the seller's signal and the buyer's belief-driven response.

Mistakes To Avoid

The mistake is to solve only strategies and treat beliefs as commentary. In PBE, beliefs are part of the equilibrium object.

Students often get stuck by saying “the player believes the type” as if belief and type were the same thing. One is the hidden state; the other is someone’s uncertain view of it. Students often get stuck by solving the strategy profile and treating beliefs as commentary. In PBE, beliefs are part of the equilibrium data, especially because off-path beliefs can affect credibility.

How To Recognize This Later

Look for any model where behavior depends on values, costs, quality, or information that only one side observes. Look for dynamic asymmetric-information models with off-path beliefs, updating, and sequential rationality.

Concepts In This Lecture

Types And Beliefs

Problem: The problem is keeping track of hidden facts and what players think those facts might be.

Mathematical handle: The object is a type space plus probability beliefs over types or histories.

What breaks without it: Without types and beliefs, private-information models would not say who knows what or how uncertainty enters payoffs. The analyst could not distinguish a high-value bidder from a low-value bidder or a receiver belief from sender truth.

Open concept page

Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium

Problem: The problem is dynamic play with hidden information: after observing actions, players must update beliefs and continue choosing rationally.

Mathematical handle: The object is a strategy profile plus a belief system, with sequential rationality and belief consistency where Bayes' rule applies.

What breaks without it: Without PBE, signaling and other dynamic asymmetric-information models could prescribe actions without explaining the beliefs that make those actions rational. Credibility would be separated from learning.
