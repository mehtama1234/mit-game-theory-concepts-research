Lecture 13: Infinitely Repeated Games

Removes the known last round and turns future punishment into a possible enforcement mechanism, where ongoing relationships can change one-shot incentives.

Open YouTube lecture

What This Lecture Teaches

The lecture removes the known final period and makes the future a possible enforcement device. Cooperation can survive when the discounted loss from punishment outweighs today's temptation.

Where The Math Enters

The math enters through discounted payoff sums and incentive constraints. A player compares the one-period deviation gain against the present value of future punishment.

The mathematical object has to exist because ongoing relationships make future consequences part of current payoff. The discount factor and continuation payoff let the course compare today’s temptation with tomorrow’s punishment. Without that object, “the future matters” would remain a slogan rather than an inequality.

Worked Mini-Example

A firm can cut price today and steal customers, but rivals may punish with low prices afterward. Cooperation works only if the firm values future profit enough.

Mistakes To Avoid

The mistake is to say indefinite repetition creates trust by itself. The lecture's claim is an inequality: patience and punishment must make deviation unattractive.

Students often get stuck by thinking infinite repetition guarantees cooperation. If players are impatient, cannot observe deviations, or cannot punish credibly, the cooperative path may fail.

How To Recognize This Later

Look for trigger strategies, continuation values, and inequalities comparing temptation today to punishment tomorrow.

Concepts In This Lecture

Infinitely Repeated Games

Problem: The problem is how cooperation can survive when each player is tempted to grab a short-run gain by cheating.

Mathematical handle: The object is a repeated game with continuation payoffs and a discount factor that measures patience.

What breaks without it: Without infinite repetition, the course would lack the clean model where ongoing relationships sustain behavior that fails in a one-shot interaction. It would also miss why patience is a strategic parameter.
