Lecture 20: Ad Auctions

Moves auction design into platform markets where rank, clicks, quality, and advertiser value interact.

Open YouTube lecture

What This Lecture Teaches

The lecture moves auction theory into platform markets. The object being allocated is attention or expected clicks across positions, so bids interact with ranking, quality, and click probabilities.

Where The Math Enters

The math enters through position values, click-through rates, bids, quality adjustments, and payment rules. A platform ranks ads by a score rather than by value alone.

The mathematical object has to exist because platform auctions allocate attention through rankings, clicks, and quality adjustments. A simple highest-bid object misses the product being sold. The ad-auction model keeps bid, position, click probability, and value in the same incentive calculation.

Equation Walkthroughs

Auction Expected Payment

Worked Mini-Example

An advertiser may value a click highly but have a low expected click rate. The platform's rule must decide whether that advertiser deserves a top slot compared with a more clickable ad.

Mistakes To Avoid

The mistake is to treat ad auctions as ordinary object auctions. The platform allocates probabilistic attention, and the mechanism must account for user response as well as advertiser bids.

Students often get stuck by seeing only bids. In sponsored search, the highest bid may not create the highest platform value if relevance and click probability differ.

How To Recognize This Later

Look for markets with ranked slots, click probabilities, quality scores, and generalized second-price style rules.

Concepts In This Lecture

Ad Auctions

Problem: The problem is selling attention slots, not a single physical object. Position affects clicks, quality affects usefulness, and bids interact with platform rules.

Mathematical handle: The object is a sponsored-search mechanism combining bids, click-through rates, quality scores, allocation order, and per-click payments.

What breaks without it: Without ad auctions, the course would leave mechanism design at textbook allocation. It would miss how online markets mix bidding strategy with measurement, ranking rules, and platform incentives.
