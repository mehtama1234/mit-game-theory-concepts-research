Signaling

Using an action or message to convey private information when others update beliefs from it.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is how someone with private information can convince others of it through an observable action.

Why does this problem exist?

Signaling exists because words or actions can change beliefs only if different types have different incentives or costs.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A belief is a probability distribution over states, types, or histories. The useful relation is `E[u(a, theta)] = sum_theta p(theta) u(a, theta).`. In plain language: theta is the unknown type or state; p(theta) is its probability; expected utility averages payoffs across possibilities.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose. The receiver's belief update is therefore just as important as the sender's action.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. Separating and pooling are the two core patterns. In separating equilibria, different types choose different signals; in pooling equilibria, signals fail to distinguish types.

Worked Mini-Example

Education can signal worker ability if high-ability workers find education less costly. The diploma matters not because it directly produces all value, but because it separates types under the right incentives.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to assume any costly action is a signal. It signals only if costs and incentives differ across types in a way receivers can reason from.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for actions that are valuable mainly because observers infer hidden quality, commitment, or type from them.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. Signaling is costly communication through action. The central question is why a low type would not imitate a high type, and the answer must come from different costs or benefits across types.
