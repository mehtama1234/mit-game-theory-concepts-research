Infinitely Repeated Games

A relationship with no fixed last round, where future punishment can shape present behavior.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is how cooperation can survive when each player is tempted to grab a short-run gain by cheating.

Why does this problem exist?

Infinite repetition exists because no fixed last round means future punishment can always matter. The future becomes a strategic asset.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A discount factor delta weights future payoffs in an intertemporal sum. The useful relation is `U = u_0 + delta u_1 + delta^2 u_2 + ...`. In plain language: u_t is payoff in period t; delta between 0 and 1 controls how strongly the future matters.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version removes the known last round so the future can discipline the present. A player considering a deviation compares the one-period gain from cheating with the discounted stream of losses after punishment. Cooperation is not assumed; it must satisfy that inequality. The model often means an ongoing relationship or no known final date, not a metaphysical claim that play literally lasts forever.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is a present value comparison. Future payoffs are weighted by powers of the discount factor. A trigger strategy works only when the discounted punishment loss is large enough to offset the immediate deviation gain.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because ongoing relationships make future consequences part of current payoff. The discount factor and continuation payoff let the course compare today’s temptation with tomorrow’s punishment. Without that object, “the future matters” would remain a slogan rather than an inequality.

Why is this concept important?

Infinitely repeated games matter because they show how the future can become an enforcement device. Cooperation can be rational when the discounted loss from future punishment is larger than the immediate gain from cheating.

What breaks without it?

Without infinite repetition, the course would lack the clean model where ongoing relationships sustain behavior that fails in a one-shot interaction. It would also miss why patience is a strategic parameter.

Worked Mini-Example

A firm may want to undercut a cartel price today, but if that triggers years of price wars, the short-run gain may not be worth it.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by thinking infinite repetition guarantees cooperation. If players are impatient, cannot observe deviations, or cannot punish credibly, the cooperative path may fail.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is enforceability of the future. Infinite repetition does not help if punishment cannot be observed, is not credible, or is discounted too heavily. The lecture use is therefore conditional: first identify the continuation value, then ask whether the proposed punishment can actually be carried out. If either piece is missing, the phrase repeated relationship is not enough to support cooperation. The lecture also uses this topic to separate mathematical infinity from economic continuation. What matters for incentives is not a calendar that literally never ends, but the absence of a commonly known final moment at which punishment loses force.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to say infinite means literally forever. The model often means no known last round or a continuing relationship with future value.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for trigger strategies, continuation values, and inequalities comparing temptation today to punishment tomorrow.

Connected Concepts

Infinite repetition connects finite horizons, folk theorem logic, implicit cartels, discounting, and subgame perfection. The same credibility question reappears, now stretched over an ongoing relationship.

Worked Example Card

Setup: Two firms expect to face each other indefinitely.

Walkthrough: A firm can gain today by cutting price, but that may trigger future low-price punishment. The comparison is between one short-run gain and the discounted value of future losses. Cooperation survives only when the future matters enough.

Lesson: Infinite repetition makes future discipline a possible current incentive.

Trap: Do not say forever solves the problem; the discount factor and punishment credibility still decide.
