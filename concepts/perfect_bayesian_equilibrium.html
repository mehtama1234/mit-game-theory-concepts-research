Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium

An equilibrium for dynamic games with beliefs that stay consistent with observed behavior.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is dynamic play with hidden information: after observing actions, players must update beliefs and continue choosing rationally.

Why does this problem exist?

PBE exists because dynamic Bayesian games need both credible continuation behavior and believable beliefs about what has happened.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A belief is a probability distribution over states, types, or histories. The useful relation is `E[u(a, theta)] = sum_theta p(theta) u(a, theta).`. In plain language: theta is the unknown type or state; p(theta) is its probability; expected utility averages payoffs across possibilities.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version combines two repairs: dynamic credibility and belief consistency. In a signaling or other dynamic private-information game, a player’s later action depends on what they believe after observing earlier behavior. PBE therefore includes both strategies and a belief system. Strategies must be sequentially rational given beliefs, and beliefs must follow Bayes’ rule wherever the observed path gives probabilities to update from.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is a coupled object. Beliefs determine optimal continuation actions, while strategies determine which observations are likely and therefore how beliefs should update. Neither side can be checked in isolation.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because dynamic private-information games require beliefs and actions to discipline each other. Strategies say what players do; beliefs say what they infer after histories. PBE binds the two so credibility and updating are checked together.

Why is this concept important?

PBE matters because dynamic private-information games need two disciplines at once: players must act optimally after histories, and their beliefs about hidden types must be consistent with observed behavior where possible.

What breaks without it?

Without PBE, signaling and other dynamic asymmetric-information models could prescribe actions without explaining the beliefs that make those actions rational. Credibility would be separated from learning.

Worked Mini-Example

In a signaling game, seeing someone choose education may change an employer's belief about worker type. PBE checks both the education choice and the employer's updated response.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by solving the strategy profile and treating beliefs as commentary. In PBE, beliefs are part of the equilibrium data, especially because off-path beliefs can affect credibility.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is off-path freedom. PBE disciplines beliefs where Bayes rule applies, but unexpected histories can still leave multiple belief specifications and multiple equilibria. The practical reading rule is to inspect both halves of the candidate equilibrium. If the strategies look sensible but the beliefs are arbitrary, or the beliefs look plausible but the continuation actions are not optimal, the PBE claim has not been earned. Belief discipline is therefore not optional decoration. The lecture uses that discipline to keep inference and sequential choice tied together.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to focus only on strategies and forget beliefs. In hidden-information dynamic games, beliefs are part of the equilibrium object.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for dynamic asymmetric-information models with off-path beliefs, updating, and sequential rationality.

Connected Concepts

PBE connects extensive form, Bayesian games, types and beliefs, signaling, and subgame perfection. It is where the course asks credibility and inference to pass the same test.

Worked Example Card

Setup: An employer observes education and updates beliefs about worker ability.

Walkthrough: A proposed strategy says which types choose education and how the employer responds. Beliefs must be updated from the observed education choice where Bayes rule applies. Then each type and the employer must find their choices optimal under those beliefs.

Lesson: PBE combines sequential credibility with disciplined belief updating.

Trap: Do not choose beliefs only to rescue a story; on-path beliefs must follow from the proposed strategies.

Equation Walkthroughs

Bayes Rule explains belief updating after observed play, Backward Induction explains sequential credibility, and Expected Utility explains optimal choice under those beliefs. PBE needs all three to keep dynamic information games disciplined.
