Cheap Talk

Costless messages that matter only when incentives make them credible enough to influence beliefs.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is whether costless messages can influence behavior when the speaker may want to mislead.

Why does this problem exist?

Cheap talk exists because communication has no direct cost or binding force, so credibility must come from aligned incentives rather than from the message itself.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A belief is a probability distribution over states, types, or histories. The useful relation is `E[u(a, theta)] = sum_theta p(theta) u(a, theta).`. In plain language: theta is the unknown type or state; p(theta) is its probability; expected utility averages payoffs across possibilities.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose. The lecture point is that speech can be strategic even when it is free.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. The interesting case is partial alignment. If interests are perfectly opposed, talk often collapses; if they are close enough, messages can partition information usefully.

Worked Mini-Example

A restaurant reviewer saying this is good may be useful if their incentives match yours. A seller saying trust me may not be informative if every seller would say it.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to say cheap means irrelevant. Costless speech can matter when incentives make truthful communication self-enforcing.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for models where messages are nonbinding and the key question is whether incentives allow information transmission.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. Cheap talk is the opposite stress test from signaling: if messages cost nothing and bind no one, only aligned incentives can make them informative. The message works when lying would not help enough.
