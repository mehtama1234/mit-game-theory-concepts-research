Rationalizability

A strategy is plausible if it can be optimal for some reasonable belief about others.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is deciding which strategies can be defended as sensible without requiring everyone to land on one exact equilibrium.

Why does this problem exist?

This exists because a strategy can be rational under some belief even if it is not part of the final equilibrium prediction. The course needs a wider plausibility test than Nash equilibrium.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A belief is a probability distribution over states, types, or histories. The useful relation is `E[u(a, theta)] = sum_theta p(theta) u(a, theta).`. In plain language: theta is the unknown type or state; p(theta) is its probability; expected utility averages payoffs across possibilities.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. This is why rationalizability is useful pedagogically: it tells the reader what rational choice alone can rule out before the stronger mutual-consistency demand of equilibrium is imposed.

Worked Mini-Example

A move that looks odd may still be rational if the player believes the opponent is likely to choose a certain action. Rationalizability asks whether such a belief can make the move best.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to treat rationalizable as meaning correct or predicted. It means defensible under some belief, not uniquely expected.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for models that want to separate impossible behavior from behavior that is merely one of several plausible responses.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. Rationalizability sits between dominance and Nash. It is stricter than allowing every action, but looser than requiring a single mutually consistent equilibrium profile.
