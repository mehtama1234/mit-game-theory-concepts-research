Backward Induction

Solving a sequential game by starting at the end.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that early choices in a tree depend on what later players will actually do, not what they announce they might do.

Why does this problem exist?

Backward induction exists because the future constrains the present. If we can solve the last decision first, that answer becomes an input into the previous decision.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Backward induction assigns optimal choices from terminal decision nodes back to the start. The useful relation is `V(node) = max_action V(next node).`. In plain language: V is continuation value; at each decision point the player chooses the next branch with the best continuation value.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version begins at the end because the end is where future speculation stops. At a final decision node, the moving player simply chooses the best available branch. Once that branch is known, the earlier node can treat the solved continuation as a value. Repeating that step back to the root gives a path through the tree. The method teaches that an early action is rational only after the later consequences it anticipates are themselves rational.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is recursion over continuation values. A node’s value is determined by the best value available at the next nodes for the player moving there. The game is solved by replacing future subtrees with their strategic value.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because sequential choice has nested future problems inside current decisions. A continuation value lets the solved future replace a whole subtree. Without that object, the analyst would have to carry every possible future story instead of using the later incentive calculation.

Why is this concept important?

Backward induction matters because sequential rationality starts at the end. The course uses it to show that earlier decisions already contain beliefs about later incentives, so solving forward without checking the end can validate empty threats.

What breaks without it?

Without backward induction, a game tree would be a picture of possible histories but not a method for evaluating them. The model could accept plans that later players would abandon once their decision node is reached.

Worked Mini-Example

If a parent threatens a punishment they will not want to impose later, a child may ignore it. Backward induction exposes that by checking the later choice first.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by reading the tree chronologically. Players live forward, but the analyst solves backward because later incentives determine which earlier expectations are credible.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is the tree. Backward induction is only as good as the modeled histories, payoffs, and information. Hidden information or imperfect observation requires richer tools. The boundary becomes visible when real players have limited foresight or when the tree is not common knowledge. The course model assumes the relevant future choices are understood well enough to be folded backward.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to reason from the start while treating later threats as fixed facts.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for finite sequential games where the last move can be solved and folded back into earlier choices.

Connected Concepts

Backward induction connects extensive form to subgame perfection, bargaining, finite repetition, and one-shot deviation. It is the recursion primitive behind the course treatment of time.
