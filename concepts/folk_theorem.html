Folk Theorem

A result showing that many cooperative outcomes can be sustained when the future matters enough.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is explaining why repeated interaction can support many outcomes, not just the one-shot equilibrium.

Why does this problem exist?

The folk theorem exists because patient players can use future punishment to make many feasible payoff promises credible.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A discount factor delta weights future payoffs in an intertemporal sum. The useful relation is `U = u_0 + delta u_1 + delta^2 u_2 + ...`. In plain language: u_t is payoff in period t; delta between 0 and 1 controls how strongly the future matters.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version shows that patient repeated games can sustain a wide set of outcomes. The target payoff must be feasible from stage-game actions and individually rational relative to the punishment each player can guarantee. If those conditions hold and players care enough about the future, equilibrium strategies can often support the target through rewards and punishments. The result is powerful because it expands what is possible, but it also weakens uniqueness.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is incentive compatibility over continuation payoffs. A proposed payoff vector must give each player enough value that deviation plus punishment is worse than staying on the path. The theorem describes a set, not a single prediction.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because repeated games can support sets of payoffs rather than one obvious outcome. Feasibility, individual rationality, and patience define which payoff vectors can be sustained. The theorem needs that set-based object to explain both possibility and multiplicity.

Why is this concept important?

The folk theorem matters because it reveals both the power and the looseness of repeated interaction. When players are patient, many payoff patterns can be sustained, so repeated-game logic can explain cooperation but may not uniquely predict which cooperation occurs.

What breaks without it?

Without the folk theorem, students may think repeated games merely select one cooperative equilibrium. The theorem shows a much larger set of feasible, individually rational outcomes supported by punishment.

Worked Mini-Example

If two firms can both earn more by keeping prices high, the folk theorem logic says this can be sustained if cheating leads to harsh enough future competition and the firms are patient.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by reading the folk theorem as “cooperation happens.” The deeper message is multiplicity: many outcomes can happen, so institutions and history may be needed to select one.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is prediction. The folk theorem shows many payoffs can be sustained; it does not by itself select which one will occur in a specific institution. The boundary is selection. The theorem tells the course that many outcomes can be made stable, but a real application still needs institutions, norms, history, or communication to explain why one equilibrium rather than another is chosen.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to read the folk theorem as predicting one outcome. It often says many outcomes are possible, which is powerful but also less predictive.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for repeated-game papers showing a large set of sustainable payoffs under patience and punishment.

Connected Concepts

The folk theorem connects infinite repetition, discounting, punishment, and cartel applications. It also motivates institutional detail because equilibrium multiplicity often needs context to choose among possible outcomes.
