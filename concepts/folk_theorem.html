Folk Theorem

A result showing that many cooperative outcomes can be sustained when the future matters enough.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is explaining why repeated interaction can support many outcomes, not just the one-shot equilibrium.

Why does this problem exist?

The folk theorem exists because patient players can use future punishment to make many feasible payoff promises credible.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A discount factor delta weights future payoffs in an intertemporal sum. The useful relation is `U = u_0 + delta u_1 + delta^2 u_2 + ...`. In plain language: u_t is payoff in period t; delta between 0 and 1 controls how strongly the future matters.

Why is this concept important?

This concept matters because it turns a loose strategic story into a checkable claim about what someone would actually choose.

What breaks without it?

Without this concept, the analysis can name the players and choices but cannot tell whether the proposed behavior survives individual incentives. A good reading of the theorem always asks what punishment is available and believable. Without a credible punishment path, the set of sustainable outcomes shrinks.

Worked Mini-Example

If two firms can both earn more by keeping prices high, the folk theorem logic says this can be sustained if cheating leads to harsh enough future competition and the firms are patient.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to read the folk theorem as predicting one outcome. It often says many outcomes are possible, which is powerful but also less predictive.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for repeated-game papers showing a large set of sustainable payoffs under patience and punishment.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. The folk theorem is both encouraging and unsettling. It shows repeated interaction can support cooperation, but it also says the model may permit many equilibria, so extra institutional detail may be needed for prediction.
