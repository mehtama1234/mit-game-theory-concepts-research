Bargaining

Dividing a surplus when each side can accept, reject, or wait.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is dividing gains from agreement when each side would like more of the surplus and can sometimes wait, reject, or walk away.

Why does this problem exist?

Bargaining exists because conflict and cooperation are intertwined. There is value to agreement, but disagreement over division changes each side's threat point.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A payoff function u_i(a_i, a_-i) maps everyone's actions to player i's payoff. The useful relation is `u_i(a_i, a_-i).`. In plain language: i is one player; a_i is that player's action; a_-i means everyone else's actions; u_i is the payoff assigned to the whole action profile.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version treats negotiation as strategic surplus division. The parties may both prefer agreement to disagreement, but they disagree about the split. Offers, rejections, delay, outside options, and patience become strategic variables. The course uses bargaining to show that power is not merely personality; it often comes from credible alternatives, time costs, and information about the other side’s willingness to wait.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is payoff mapping plus continuation value. Each offer determines a division if accepted and a future subgame if rejected. The value of waiting or walking away sets the pressure on today’s demand.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because surplus division depends on strategic alternatives, not only on the size of the surplus. Offers, rejection payoffs, delay costs, and outside options must be represented together. Bargaining models create that object so division can be explained by incentives rather than asserted fairness.

Why is this concept important?

Bargaining matters because many strategic problems are neither pure conflict nor pure cooperation. There is surplus from agreement, but the division of that surplus depends on patience, outside options, timing, and information.

What breaks without it?

Without bargaining theory, the course could say agreement is efficient but not explain who gets what or why delay occurs. It would miss how threat points and waiting costs shape the split.

Worked Mini-Example

If a seller values a car at 8,000 and a buyer values it at 10,000, trade creates 2,000 of surplus. Bargaining asks who captures that surplus and why.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by assuming the fair split is the predicted split. Game theory asks what division survives incentives, not what division sounds fair from outside the model.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is the source of bargaining power. If outside options, patience, information, or commitment are modeled wrongly, the predicted split can look precise while resting on fragile inputs. The boundary matters in applications because observed delay can come from strategic waiting, private information, transaction costs, or changing outside options. The bargaining model clarifies one mechanism, but the analyst still has to identify which pressure is active.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to treat bargaining power as a vague personality trait when the model usually locates it in patience, outside options, timing, or information.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for surplus division, alternating offers, take-it-or-leave-it offers, or incomplete information about values.

Connected Concepts

Bargaining connects payoff mapping to backward induction, discounting, and private information. It also links to mechanisms because rules and outside options shape what agreements are incentive-compatible.

Worked Example Card

Setup: A buyer values a car at 10000 and a seller values keeping it at 8000.

Walkthrough: Trade creates 2000 of surplus because the buyer values the car more than the seller does. Bargaining does not ask whether trade is possible; it asks how that surplus is divided. Patience, outside options, and timing shape the split.

Lesson: Bargaining separates the existence of gains from trade from the fight over who captures them.

Trap: Do not confuse total surplus with each side's payoff.

Equation Walkthroughs

The Discounted Payoff Sum card explains why time itself changes bargaining power. Waiting is not free: future surplus is converted into present value, so delay can make a formally larger future payoff less attractive.
