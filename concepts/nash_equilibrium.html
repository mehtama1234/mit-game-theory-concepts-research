Nash Equilibrium

A profile of strategies where no player wants to change alone.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is making a prediction that does not immediately fall apart when each participant asks, given what the others are doing, should I switch?

Why does this problem exist?

Nash equilibrium exists because a strategic prediction has to be self-consistent. If one player wants to change after seeing the proposed profile, the profile was not a stable prediction.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Equilibrium requires each strategy to be a best response to the others. The useful relation is `a_i in BR_i(a_-i) for every player i.`. In plain language: For each player i, their chosen action a_i must belong to the set of best responses against the other players' actions.

Why is this concept important?

Nash matters because it is the baseline language for stable strategic prediction throughout the rest of the course.

What breaks without it?

Without Nash equilibrium, the course could list incentives but would lack the basic test for whether a proposed strategic outcome can hold together.

Worked Mini-Example

At a four-way stop, everyone going at once is not stable if each driver would rather wait or avoid collision given the others. A stable traffic convention must make each driver's move sensible given the convention.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to hear equilibrium and think socially best. Nash can describe bad outcomes, including prisoner's dilemma, because it is about unilateral incentives, not collective welfare.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for claims that a behavior pattern persists because no individual participant can gain by deviating alone.

Connected Concepts

The same primitive returns whenever the course asks whether a proposed behavior is compatible with incentives, beliefs, timing, or information. The concept also teaches humility: a Nash outcome may be inefficient, unequal, or fragile under changed assumptions. Its claim is narrower and sharper: no one player can profit by changing alone while the rest stay fixed.
