Strategic Form Games

A compact table of players, actions, and payoffs chosen at the same time.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is representing a situation where everyone chooses without first seeing the others' choices, yet everyone's payoff depends on the whole combination.

Why does this problem exist?

This exists because strategic dependence needs a shared object. A verbal story hides who can choose what and what each combination produces.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

A payoff function u_i(a_i, a_-i) maps everyone's actions to player i's payoff. The useful relation is `u_i(a_i, a_-i).`. In plain language: i is one player; a_i is that player's action; a_-i means everyone else's actions; u_i is the payoff assigned to the whole action profile.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version makes the strategic situation flat and inspectable. A strategic-form game lists players, their available actions, and the payoff for each complete action profile. This representation is powerful because it forces the analyst to stop describing one player’s choice alone. Every payoff cell already contains the interaction: my action and your action together. Once the table exists, dominance, best response, Nash equilibrium, mixed strategies, and zero-sum reasoning can all be asked as precise questions about rows, columns, and cells.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is a function from action profiles to payoffs. The input is everyone’s action, not one action by itself. The output can be different for each player, which is why the same cell can be attractive to one player and unattractive to another.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because strategic dependence needs a container. If each payoff depends on everyone’s action, the model needs a way to list the whole action profile and the payoff consequences together. Strategic form is the simplest object that keeps those dependencies visible and checkable.

Why is this concept important?

Strategic form matters because it gives interdependence a minimal representation. By putting each player, action, and payoff in one table, the course can ask dominance, best-response, and Nash questions without losing track of whose choice affects whom.

What breaks without it?

Without strategic form, simultaneous games would remain verbal scenarios. Students could argue about incentives, but they would lack the structured object needed to compare rows, columns, and deviations cleanly.

Worked Mini-Example

In prisoner's dilemma, each person chooses confess or stay silent. The payoff in a cell is not caused by one choice alone; it is caused by the pair of choices.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by reading a row as if it has a single value. A row has several consequences, one for each possible opponent action, so a strategy cannot be judged until the contingency is named.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is timing and information. Strategic form is clean for simultaneous or reduced games, but it hides histories and observations unless those are folded into strategies with care.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to read one row or one column as if the other player were irrelevant. The whole point of the table is joint dependence.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for simultaneous moves, payoff matrices, or models where the exact timing is suppressed so strategic dependence can be seen cleanly.

Connected Concepts

Strategic form supports dominance, iterated deletion, best response, Nash, mixed strategies, and zero-sum analysis. Later trees and Bayesian games are extensions of this representation when timing or hidden information must be shown explicitly.
