Extensive Form Games

A game drawn as a tree so timing and information can be represented.

What real-world problem is this about?

The problem is that some games unfold over time. A later choice can depend on an earlier choice, and some players may know more about the history than others.

Why does this problem exist?

This exists because timing and information change incentives. A threat that sounds good at the start may not be attractive when the threatened moment arrives.

What is the mathematical idea underneath?

Backward induction assigns optimal choices from terminal decision nodes back to the start. The useful relation is `V(node) = max_action V(next node).`. In plain language: V is continuation value; at each decision point the player chooses the next branch with the best continuation value.

Lecture-Depth Walkthrough

The lecture-depth version asks what the table hides. If players move in sequence, observe earlier actions, or choose after reaching different histories, the game needs a tree. The tree records decision nodes, branches, histories, payoffs at terminal nodes, and information sets when a player cannot distinguish where they are. This representation is not visual decoration. It is what lets the course ask whether a threat is credible, whether a later player observes an earlier choice, and whether beliefs must be updated after a signal.

Mathematical Intuition

The mathematical intuition is recursive structure. A node summarizes a history, an action selects a branch, and a terminal path gives payoffs. The tree turns time into an object that backward induction and subgame perfection can operate on.

Why This Mathematical Object Has To Exist

The mathematical object has to exist because time and information change what an action means. A flat list of actions cannot say who moved first, what was observed, or which histories a player can distinguish. The tree structure preserves those facts so later credibility and belief questions have a place to attach.

Why is this concept important?

Extensive form matters because timing can change strategy. A player who moves later may observe history, respond to threats, or condition action on earlier choices, and those possibilities cannot be represented honestly in a flat simultaneous table.

What breaks without it?

Without extensive form, the course could mistake a sequential conflict for a simultaneous one. That would hide credibility, information sets, and the fact that later choices can make earlier announcements meaningful or empty.

Worked Mini-Example

In an entry game, an incumbent may threaten a price war after entry. The tree asks whether fighting is actually optimal once entry has happened.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often get stuck by assuming a tree automatically means everyone observed every earlier move. Information sets matter because the same physical history and the same known history are not always the same thing.

Where The Idea Stops Working

The boundary is representational honesty. A tree can only support credible conclusions if its nodes, information sets, and payoffs capture what players actually know and can choose.

Common Misunderstanding

The mistake is to collapse every sequential game into a payoff matrix and lose the credibility problem.

How to Recognize This in a New Paper or Model

Look for histories, decision nodes, information sets, or any claim where the order of moves changes what behavior can be believed.

Connected Concepts

Extensive form connects directly to backward induction, subgame perfection, bargaining, one-shot deviation, and PBE. It is the representation that lets credibility and belief updating become mathematical rather than rhetorical.

Worked Example Card

Setup: An incumbent firm threatens a price war if a new firm enters.

Walkthrough: A flat table can record the threat, but a tree asks when the threat would actually be chosen. After entry happens, the incumbent reaches a later decision node. If fighting loses money at that node, the threat is not credible.

Lesson: The tree matters because timing changes what a promise or threat means.

Trap: Do not assume a drawn branch is a believable future action; later incentives still have to be checked.

Equation Walkthroughs

The Backward Induction card explains why a game tree is more than a picture. Once choices happen over time, each earlier branch gets its meaning from the continuation choices that later players will actually want to make.
